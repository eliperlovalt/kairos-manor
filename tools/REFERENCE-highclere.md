# The Highclere reference (relayed by KAIROS, 27 Nov 2026, at His Grace's wish that the interiors read like Downton Abbey)

For the dressing passes: state rooms first, then below stairs, then bedrooms. The Estate keeps its own architecture; this is how rooms are furnished, finished, lit and coloured. Where the Estate has a room not listed, use the nearest. KAIROS marked his sources as verified or as period convention; this file is his summary, kept as he sent it.

Scale: state rooms tall (5 to 6 blocks); the Saloon or great hall double that, with a gallery; servants' rooms 3 to 4; bedrooms 4 to 5.

| # | Room | What makes it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Saloon, Great Hall | Biggest room, double height, a gallery on three sides. Stone or chequered marble floor, rugs only at the fire end. Linenfold panelling with shields, gilded leather above. Dark hammerbeam roof. A big stone fireplace with arms over it. Few heavy carved pieces, the middle kept open, a tall clock, settles. A lantern or chandelier and wall brackets. Stone grey, oak, antique gold, oxblood, black iron. |
| 2 | Library | Long, in two halves divided by columns. Mahogany bookcases floor to ceiling, a gilded coffered ceiling, a central library table, armchairs by the fire, reading stands, a globe, a ladder, a desk by a window, green-shaded lamps. One bookcase is a hidden door to the Music Room. Mahogany, leather brown, gold leaf, claret, cream. |
| 3 | Drawing Room | Large and tall. Green silk above white panelling, a white and gilt ceiling, a huge marble chimneypiece with a portrait between two commodes. Sofas and armchairs in two or three groups with small tables, a piano in a corner, cabinets along the walls, many small objects. A big chandelier, sconces. Sage green, white, gold, rose, cream. |
| 4 | Dining Room | A long rectangle, a table for up to thirty, a carpet under it with a bare border. Warm yellow silk, a large equestrian portrait and family portraits. Sideboards, a serving table, a screen hiding the service door. A chandelier over the table, candles on it. Butter yellow, mahogany, silver, deep red, white linen. |
| 5 | Music Room | Medium, light. Painted panels and embroideries, a painted ceiling, a mirror over the mantel. A grand piano, a harp, gilt chairs, a writing desk, candelabra. Gilt, pale blue, ivory, soft red. |
| 6 | Morning Room | Small, east or south light, pale paper. A writing desk at the window, a small sofa, two armchairs, a work table, flowers. Primrose, pale blue, white, rose. |
| 7 | Smoking Room | Dark panelling or stamped leather, game paintings, a big fireplace with a clock, leather armchairs and a Chesterfield, a drinks table, a card table, low warm lamps. Oxblood, tobacco, dark green, brass. |
| 8 | Billiard Room | Long, one table under a low hanging light, clear space round it, raised benches along a wall, a scoreboard, a cue rack, hunting pictures, panelling to the dado. Billiard green, oak, brass, cream. |

| 10 | Principal bedroom | Large but lower than the state rooms. Silk or chintz walls, a canopied or tester bed, bedside tables, a wardrobe, a dressing table, a chaise, a screen, a washstand with jug and bowl, shutters and heavy curtains, a fire. Pale green or blue, white, rose, gilt. |
| 11 | Dressing room | Small, narrow, darker. A day bed, a wardrobe, a tallboy, a shaving stand, a boot rack, a long mirror. Navy, brown, cream, mahogany. |
| 12 | Nursery | A top-floor suite: day nursery, night nursery, nurse's room. Boards and rugs, a fireguard, an animal frieze, a big table and small chairs, a rocking horse, a toy cupboard, iron cots, a nurse's chair, barred windows. Cream, pale yellow, sky blue, red toys. |
| 13 | Servants' Hall | Stone flags, whitewash or brown and cream. A long table with benches or chairs, the butler or housekeeper at the head, a dresser, a fire, a clock over the mantel, a notice board, a plain hanging lamp. |

| 15 | Scullery | Small, wet, flags with a drain. Deep stone sinks, a plate rack, a copper boiler, pails. |

| 17 | Housekeeper's Room | Furnished like a family room: carpet, armchairs by the fire, a desk, a key cupboard, a sideboard with a tea set, a linen press. |

Ten things that make it read as a real house: two houses in one joined by a baize door; different ceiling heights; the bell board; a fire in every room with scuttle and fireguard; clutter (small tables, frames, flowers, books); wear (worn runners, a darker patch before the fire); a different material per room; long sightlines through open doors down the state rooms to a window; servants' routes (screened service doors, back stairs, side doors); signs of use (a tray set out, a coat on a hook, cards mid-game, boots at the back door).

Mistakes to hunt: furniture floating or alone in the middle (group it; tables need chairs; rugs under groups); rooms too small or too empty (layer: rug, big piece, small tables, lamps, pictures); every room the same colour; doors to nowhere; no thresholds, skirting, dado or cornice; no change at the baize door; flat bare ceilings; identical windows with no curtains; chimneys with no fireplace below; corridors as empty tubes (add a runner, a bench, a lamp, a picture).
